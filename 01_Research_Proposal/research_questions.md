# Research Questions

## Project Title

**Multimodal Deep Learning for Pediatric Brain Tumor Classification Using MRI and Clinical Information**

## Research Domain

* Healthcare Artificial Intelligence
* Medical Imaging AI
* Pediatric Neuroimaging
* Deep Learning
* Multimodal AI
* Explainable AI

## Primary Research Question

Does integrating MRI-derived features with clinically available patient information improve pediatric brain tumor classification compared with MRI-only and clinical-only approaches?

## Secondary Research Questions

1. How does the use of individual MRI sequences affect classification performance?
2. Does combining multiple MRI sequences improve classification compared with using a single MRI sequence?
3. How well can clinical information alone support the selected classification task?
4. Does multimodal MRI-clinical fusion improve classification performance compared with unimodal models?
5. How does model performance change when one or more MRI sequences are unavailable?
6. How does model performance change when clinical information is incomplete?
7. Which MRI regions and clinical variables contribute to model predictions?

## Research Hypotheses

* **H1:** A multimodal MRI-clinical model may achieve better classification performance than MRI-only and clinical-only models.
* **H2:** Combining multiple MRI sequences may improve classification performance compared with using a single sequence.
* **H3:** Training and evaluating models under missing-modality conditions may help identify approaches that are more robust to incomplete data.
* **H4:** Explainability methods may help identify image regions and clinical variables associated with model predictions.

These hypotheses will be evaluated through experiments and may not be supported by the results.

## Research Objectives

1. Investigate suitable pediatric brain MRI datasets and associated clinical information.
2. Develop an MRI-only classification baseline.
3. Develop a clinical-only classification baseline.
4. Develop a multimodal MRI-clinical classification model.
5. Compare the models using appropriate evaluation metrics.
6. Investigate performance under missing-modality conditions.
7. Analyze model predictions using suitable explainability methods.
